# Back to the laptop presence

Alex said the navigation work had become unclear and directed Briar to “get back
to what I was asking for”. This replaces that as the immediate review target:
one small, quiet face, not a dashboard or a collection of fictional projects.

Open `index.html` directly in Brave. The face sits at the bottom-right of the
page. Resize the browser window if desired. This is not an OS-level desktop
widget and does not float above other applications or change system settings.

The blue light clusters drift and gently expand continuously, occasionally
suggesting an abstract face. Each opening varies the light arrangement and drift;
face gatherings recur with varied timing and strength rather than a fixed replay.
Version 3 adds a visible animation clock, explicit pause/error reasons and a
Restart motion button. It also follows real elapsed foreground time when browser
frame delivery is throttled. These help distinguish a rendering failure, an
intentional pause and slow frame delivery; none proves the reported browser
failure has been reproduced. Restart explicitly selects Quiet blue and enables
motion, overriding a still setting only in response to that button click.
Version 4 increases cluster travel, changes width and height independently and
adds clearer internal light movement. Alex confirmed the animation clock kept
advancing while the face appeared stopped; this iteration addresses visibility
of movement rather than claiming the animation loop had stopped.
Version 5 removes the oval rim, glass and clipping mask. Lights define their own
soft, irregular edge. Individual lights now follow independent smoothly joined
random waypoints at varied speeds and distances; clusters also change shape
irregularly instead of all breathing in and out. Face gatherings temporarily
reduce wandering to preserve the emerging expression. Still modes remain still.
Click the face for a short explanation. Preview controls are tucked inside that
explanation, not shown alongside it: quiet, attending, focused work, concern,
urgency, waiting and unknown/disconnected. These are manually chosen previews,
not live states. There are no projects, invented alerts, percentage progress,
accounts, microphone, camera, storage or network requests.

Waiting, unknown/disconnected and reduced-motion modes are still. Background
tabs stop animating. Foreground drawing is limited to about 12 frames per second;
this is not a measurement of battery use or a production rendering decision.

The material follows the accepted clustered-light direction. The purpose of this
round is to make the intended experience tangible again. It does not establish
live status, intuition, peripheral comfort, accessibility or browser acceptance.
Hardware, VR, actual harness integration and multi-user integration remain future
work requiring their own agreed scope. Earlier studies remain preserved.

Version 6 adds a draggable Briar-name handle, arrow-key movement (Shift for
larger steps), viewport containment and an Open small window button. That button
requests a separate 260×460 browser window with no opener access. Browser popup
policy may block it or open a tab instead. Move the separate window by its OS
title bar; it is not guaranteed always-on-top and its background is not
transparent. Position is session-only and is not stored. No service is started.
