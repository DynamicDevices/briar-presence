# Visual references

## Lost in Space (Netflix, 2018–2021)

- [Spectral Motion / Stan Winston School: constructing the robot](https://www.stanwinstonschool.com/blog/how-spectral-motion-artists-built-robot-for-netflix-series-lost-in-space)
  describes the convex dome, remotely operated rear-projected imagery, red tones
  when angry and softer colours when calm. Some projected imagery was replaced
  digitally, including when sunlight washed it out.
- [FutureWorks: bringing the robot to life](https://www.futureworks.in/case-studies/VFX-lost-in-space-Netflix)
  describes moving blue lights becoming red during aggression, and the challenge
  of making 2D face graphics appear volumetric.

These production accounts establish the construction and broad colour meaning.
They do not supply a measured animation specification, a blue-to-red timing curve
or evidence that a particular interface meaning will be intuitive to our users.

Alex prefers the groupings of lights rather than the first prototype's drawn
lines. Briar's concern/urgency semantics differ deliberately from the show's
aggression signal. The studies contain original procedural lights, not copied
stills, textures, footage, sound or traced face graphics.
